# IEEE Transactions Abstract and Contribution Paragraph Guide

Use this reference when drafting or rebuilding an IEEE Transactions Abstract, Introduction closing paragraph, or contribution summary for robotics, automation, control, communications, and industrial informatics.

## Core principle

An IEEE Transactions summary is a technical funnel, not a broad-audience feature paragraph:

`engineering context -> specific bottleneck -> technical gap -> proposed method/system/theory -> evidence -> bounded contribution`

The paragraph should let a reviewer identify the problem, novelty, method class, evidence, and limits without hunting through the paper.

## Abstract structure

1. Context: one sentence naming the system/task and why it matters technically.
2. Gap: one sentence stating what existing methods cannot handle.
3. Method: one sentence naming the proposed idea, model, controller, algorithm, or architecture.
4. Evidence: one to two sentences with concrete evaluation setting and quantitative results when available.
5. Bound: one sentence on scope, assumptions, deployment relevance, or limitations.

Hard IEEE constraints:

- Do not include numbered citations in the Abstract.
- Do not include numbered equations or footnotes in the Abstract.
- Define nonstandard acronyms at first use.
- Keep claims tied to evidence; do not promise generality beyond the experiments/theory.

## Introduction closing contribution paragraph

The final Introduction paragraph should state:

- the exact problem addressed
- the technical novelty
- the main theoretical/system/algorithmic contribution
- the experimental or analytical evidence
- the target constraints: latency, robustness, stability, convergence, reliability, cost, hardware limits, or industrial deployment

For T-ASE, keep this distinct from the Note to Practitioners. The contribution paragraph is technical; the NtP explains practical use and limitations for practitioners.
